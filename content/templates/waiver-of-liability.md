# Waiver of Liability + Service Acknowledgment

> **Use:** Get one signed before starting any work where there's a non-trivial risk of property damage (hedge trim over a fence, branch work over a driveway, mechanical edging near utilities, post-storm debris removal).
> **When you DO NOT need this:** Routine mowing on an empty lot. Routine edging along a curb. Routine mulching in defined beds.
> **Format:** Single-page PDF (this file). Print 25 on cotton stock to start, $15.
> **Renewal:** None — each job that needs it gets a fresh signature.

## When this is overkill

- Standing-mow on a flat, open yard with no gates/pets/structures? **No waiver needed.**
- Edging along a clean curb line, no utility markings? **No waiver needed.**
- Mulching in pre-defined beds with no plant material within 6 inches? **No waiver needed.**
- You're visiting a property for the **first time** and there's a dog in the yard? **Get the waiver.** Better to over-sign than under-sign.

The trigger is **property-damage risk in work where the consequences would be expensive.** Hedge trim over a $5,000 fence. Branch work over a driveway. Edging on the side of a utility easement. Each of these is a "get it signed" moment.

## Why a $500 self-insured cap

Until OBJ-M2-003 reactivates (insurance purchase triggered), the business operates with limited cash reserves. The $500 self-insured cap means:

- A customer knows there's a ceiling, but a reasonable one.
- You're not pretending to be a fully-insured operator.
- Anything above $500 requires the customer to file on their homeowner's insurance (which is the right place for it anyway).

When insurance kicks in: bump the self-insured cap to $1,000 (matches the deductible on a typical GL policy). The carrier handles the rest.

## Why this isn't a real waiver

This is a **service acknowledgment + limited liability acknowledgment**. Not a hold-harmless agreement. The reason: hold-harmless agreements tend to be unenforceable in Florida residential services contexts (especially when they try to waive gross negligence), and trying to use one as a solo operator sets the wrong tone.

What this document does:
- Records what was discussed
- Documents pre-existing conditions
- Sets a clear self-insured cap
- Establishes the right dispute-resolution process (a phone call before anything else)

What it doesn't do:
- Pretend to limit liability for gross negligence
- Pretend to be a substitute for actual insurance
- Allow cost-shifting for consequential damages (because courts disagree on whether you can)

## Storage

- **Signed copies** go in a single sheet-protector binder, alphabetical by last name. Spreadsheet tracks `J-XXXX-XX` with date, customer, and "Y/N signed."
- **Digitize** the binder once it hits 50 pages (scan to Google Drive `/clients/waivers/`).
- **Retention**: 5 years post-completion of the last service for that customer. After 5 years, shred.

## The waiver tone

Notice what it doesn't say:
- "I release Largo Lawn from all liability." — unenforceable.
- "I waive my right to sue." — makes customers nervous.
- "This is a binding arbitration agreement." — overkill, mismatched to a $75 mow.
- "Customer accepts full responsibility for all property." — too one-sided.

It says what was agreed to, in plain English. Customer signs it not because they fear the lawyer, but because they understand what was discussed and what's covered.
