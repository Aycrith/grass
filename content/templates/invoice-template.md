# Invoice Template — Residential Lawn-Care (Largo, FL)

> **Use:** Hand at job end OR email same-day. Always include sales-tax line (even when DR-1 is deferred — see note).
> **Tone:** Receipt-level plain. No marketing. No upsell.
> **Format:** Carbon-copy printout (Vistaprint 8.5×11 NCR, $25 for 100) OR PDF from Jobber (post-Month 2, deferred).

## Sales-tax note (deferred, but always show the line)

**Until you register with FL Dept of Revenue (DR-1)**, you should still:

1. **Always show the tax line on the invoice** at 6.75% (or the rate in effect at the time).
2. **Track tax accruals internally** — even if you don't remit them.
3. **Save 30% of every taxable payment** in a separate "tax" sub-account.

Once you cross the $1,000/mo run-rate (estimated Month 3-4 of recurring clients), register via Sunbiz DR-1 (see `drafts/sunbiz/dr-1-application.md`) and start filing Form DR-15 quarterly. Until then, the accrual approach keeps you honest without requiring the registration.

**Why defer:** Sunbiz/DOR registration is cheap and fast (no fee), but quarterly filing has a compliance overhead. Solo-operator overhead. Hold until economics justify it.

## Invoice-numbering convention

- **Q-YYYYMMDD-XX** = quote
- **I-YYYYMMDD-XX** = invoice
- **R-YYYYMMDD-XX** = receipt (when paid)
- **X-YYYYMMDD-XX** = refund or credit memo

Sequential per day, no gaps. Spreadsheet column-free. If you skip a number, the audit trail gets weird.

## When to convert to Jobber (Month 2+)

- 1-3 recurring clients: this template + a spreadsheet
- 4-10 recurring clients: still this template + a spreadsheet, or Jobber Lite ($39/mo, see `research/distribution/autonomous-paid-acquisition.md`)
- 11+ recurring clients: Jobber Core — invoicing, scheduling, payments, automatic
- Hard rule: don't pay for software if it costs more than the labor it saves

## Why no late-fee policy

Late fees are a customer-acquisition killer. If someone doesn't pay within 14 days, text them. If they still don't pay within 30 days, stop the service. No collections, no late fees, no credit reporting. The LTV math says it's cheaper to lose a 1-in-50 slow payer than to alienate the 49 who pay promptly with a hostile late-fee culture. This is consistent with the brand's "no contract" stance.
