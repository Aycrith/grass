| Season | Months | Demand profile |

**Year-round mowing** is viable because USDA zone 10a (subtropical) means bahia, St. Augustine, and Bermuda grass grow year-round (slower Dec-Feb but never dormant like northern climates).

## Hurricane prep as discrete revenue stream

Pinellas hurricane season (June-November) creates a discrete revenue event:
- Pre-storm property prep: $150-300 per visit (securing loose items, tree limb removal).
- Post-storm debris clearance: $300-800 per visit (chainsaw work, hauling).
- Estimated: 2-3 named-storm events per season = $400-1,500 incremental per active customer = $5-15K annual hurricane-prep revenue at 50 customers.

## Market risks

1. **Hurricane damage cycle** — major storm years can wipe out 10-30% of recurring customers (relocation, property damage). Mitigation: insurance rider + 1-month cash reserve.
2. **Seasonal worker competition** — large franchises (TruGreen, Massey) drop prices aggressively in shoulder seasons to maintain crew utilization. Mitigation: don't compete on price; compete on consistency + customer comm.
3. **New construction slowdown** — fewer new lawns to capture if Pinellas building permits drop. Monitor monthly.
4. **Water restrictions** — Pinellas has year-round watering restrictions; fertilization/pest bans in summer (FL-Friendly Fertilizer Rule) reduce service-line expansion options.
