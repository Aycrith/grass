# Visual Audit Report — Landing Page

> **Date:** 2026-07-17  
> **URL audited:** http://localhost:3000  
> **Viewport coverage:** Desktop (1440×900) and mobile (375×667)  
> **Method:** Interactive browser scroll audit + Playwright visual regression comparison  
> **Status:** Complete

---

## 1. Executive summary

The landing page is structurally sound and all interactive elements work, but the **hero section has a critical responsive layout bug** (headline clipping on small viewports). Beyond that, the steward's interview feedback points to deeper design-direction issues: the current warm/sand palette feels wrong for a Florida lawn-care brand, the scroll-driven mower hero reads as gimmicky, motion feels incoherent, and the conversion path is not obvious enough.

Playwright visual regression shows **all 14 routes failing against baselines**, which indicates either widespread visual drift or stale baselines. The home-route diff is driven in part by time-dependent `ScheduleTimeline` rendering (the D-0040 frozen-clock fix only applies to the test suite, not the dev server).

---

## 2. Methodology

1. **Interactive browser audit** — scrolled the full page on desktop and mobile, tested interactions (form, FAQ, CTAs, ConversionRail), and inspected for layout issues.
2. **Playwright visual regression** — ran `visual/routes.spec.ts` (chromium-desktop) to compare current renders against committed baselines.
3. **Section-by-section review** against the 9-section composition and the steward's stated concerns.

---

## 3. Critical findings

### 3.1 Hero headline clips on small viewports 🔴 Critical

**Finding:** On narrow/mobile viewports the hero headline is cut off / overlaps the scene graphics. The text is not reliably readable.

**Impact:** First impression is broken; users on phones (a major share of local-service traffic) cannot read the value proposition.

**Recommendation:**
- Audit `HeroMowerScene.module.css` for `clamp()`/`min()`/`max()` misuse on the headline.
- Ensure the headline container has a safe minimum height and `overflow: visible` where needed.
- Consider a simpler, non-scroll-driven hero layout for mobile.

---

## 4. High-priority findings

### 4.1 Hero concept feels gimmicky and on-brand color palette is wrong 🔴 High

**Finding:** The scroll-driven mower reveal was intended to be delightful, but the steward finds it confusing/gimmicky. The warm sand/cream palette does not read as a Florida lawn-care brand.

**Impact:** Brand trust and premium feel are undermined before the user scrolls.

**Recommendation:**
- Pivot to the existing `/hero-v2` **Field Telemetry** direction (real photography, kinetic type, live status widgets, passport stamp, telemetry strip).
- Refresh the palette toward lush greens, palm tones, and sun/clay accents instead of sand/cream.
- Make the headline fully visible on first paint; use scroll for secondary reveals only.

### 4.2 Motion is incoherent and non-sensical 🔴 High

**Finding:** Animations across the page do not feel like a unified system. The hero scroll-scrub, card hover tilts, fade-ups, and ConversionRail transitions use different easings/durations and do not reinforce a single story.

**Impact:** The page feels patched together rather than designed.

**Recommendation:**
- Define a shared motion language (one easing family, one duration scale, one stagger rule).
- Remove purely decorative motion (e.g., the mower scroll-scrub) if it does not serve conversion.
- Gate all motion behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### 4.3 Conversion path is unclear 🔴 High

**Finding:** The steward explicitly stated, "Users don't know what to do first." The Coverage Check form is present but not visually dominant enough, and CTAs below the hero compete for attention.

**Impact:** Lower quote-request conversion.

**Recommendation:**
- Make the primary "Get a free quote" CTA visually dominant in the hero.
- Repeat the primary CTA after each major section (ServiceBento, Pricing, Process, Schedule).
- Ensure the Coverage Check form is the obvious next step, not an optional interaction.

---

## 5. Medium-priority findings

### 5.1 Process Steps section feels faint and under-designed 🟡 Medium

**Finding:** The "01, 02..." step markers lack visual weight. The section does not feel premium.

**Recommendation:**
- Increase contrast/size of step numbers.
- Add icons or illustrations that map to each step.
- Use a stronger horizontal or vertical timeline treatment.

### 5.2 Schedule Timeline is information-heavy and conversion-light 🟡 Medium

**Finding:** The weekly route / field log section is clear but adds cognitive load. The conversion intent (book this day) is present but could be stronger.

**Recommendation:**
- Simplify the default view; hide the month calendar by default (it already is, but the toggle could be subtler).
- Elevate the "Book this mow" CTA visually.
- Consider collapsing the week strip into a single "next available day" card on mobile.

### 5.3 Visual regression baselines are stale across all routes 🟡 Medium

**Finding:** `bunx playwright test visual/routes.spec.ts` fails for all 14 routes. The home-route diff is partly due to `ScheduleTimeline` rendering with the current wall-clock time instead of the frozen test time.

**Impact:** The CI visual-guard is currently noisy and not trustworthy.

**Recommendation:**
- Refresh baselines after the hero/palette rework is complete.
- Ensure the dev server and test suite use the same time-freezing strategy (D-0040 already added `page.clock.setSystemTime` to the test specs).

---

## 6. Low-priority findings / opportunities

### 6.1 Operator stats could be more visually impactful 🟢 Low

**Finding:** The "6 years / 47 yards" stats in `OperatorStrip` are readable but do not stand out.

**Opportunity:** Use a stat-bar or large-number treatment to make trust signals more scannable.

### 6.2 ServiceBento cards are solid but could feel more premium 🟢 Low

**Finding:** Cards work well on desktop and mobile, hover states are functional, and the grid stacks correctly.

**Opportunity:** Add subtle texture, refined shadows, or a featured-card treatment to increase perceived value.

### 6.3 Pricing hierarchy is clear but could be more compelling 🟢 Low

**Finding:** Pricing tiers are easy to distinguish.

**Opportunity:** Add a "Most popular" badge, annual savings callout, or risk reversal (e.g., "Satisfaction guaranteed") to the anchor tier.

---

## 7. Mobile-specific notes

- **Hero:** Headline clipping is the main issue (see 3.1).
- **Coverage Check:** Form stacks correctly; input is usable.
- **ServiceBento:** Cards stack cleanly.
- **Pricing:** Cards stack correctly.
- **ScheduleTimeline:** No horizontal overflow, but the section is long; consider a condensed mobile view.
- **FAQAccordion:** Works as intended.
- **ConversionRail:** Visible but does not cover content.
