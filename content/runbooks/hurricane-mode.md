# Runbook 3 — Hurricane Mode Procedure

> **Use:** When a tropical storm or hurricane is approaching the Largo FL area. Hurricane season: June 1 - November 30.
> **Activation threshold:** A named storm is within 5 days of projected landfall within 100 miles of Pinellas County, OR sustained winds ≥ 30 mph are forecast within 48 hours, OR a Hurricane Watch/Warning has been issued by NHC for the area.
> **Goal:** Get customers prepared, get yourself home safe, resume service only after clearance.

---

## Pre-season setup (May, before June 1)

1. **Run hurricane prep annually.** Send template T7 to all recurring customers in mid-May. (See `content/email-templates.md`.)
2. **Stock hurricane supplies**: tarps, hand saw, pruners, work gloves, phone charger banks, water.
3. **Charge all equipment** to 100% before any forecast storm.
4. **Save the National Hurricane Center bookmark**: nhc.noaa.gov. Check daily during season.

## T-5 to T-7 days (storm in the Atlantic)

1. **Activate hurricane mode** in your task tracker.
2. **Text template S6** to all recurring customers: *"Heads up — [Storm Name] is in the Atlantic. Tracking. Will send update on [date]. No service changes yet."*
3. **Plan the next 7 days as if the storm hits on its current track.** Block time for prep work + post-storm cleanup.

## T-3 days (storm tracking toward Florida)

1. **Email template T7** to all recurring customers: full hurricane prep guide.
2. **Identify at-risk customers**: large trees near houses, hedge rows over fences, customers with hurricane prep add-on. These get priority visits.
3. **Confirm you can do T-1 prep visits.** Each customer gets ~30 minutes of prep work (branch removal, item securing, loose-object pickup).
4. **Pre-stage supplies** in the truck: tarps, tools, fuel.

## T-1 day (landfall within 24 hours, typically)

1. **Stop new customer acquisition** until post-storm. No new quotes, no new visits.
2. **Complete remaining prep visits** if conditions are safe. Skip if winds already > 20 mph sustained.
3. **Final customer text (template S7)**: *"Last update before landfall. All scheduled work paused through [clearance date]. Will check in after the storm passes."*
4. **Top off fuel** (vehicle + cans). Cash on hand for 5 days.
5. **Charge phone + batteries.** Vehicle plugged in (if EV).
6. **Secure your own property.** Family, vehicle, equipment.

## During the storm (T-0 to landfall + 24h)

1. **No outdoor work.** Period. Wind, debris, lightning.
2. **Monitor NHC + local news** for clearance updates.
3. **Field emergency customer calls.** If a customer has an emergency (e.g., a tree fell on a structure), advise them to call 911 / their insurance / a tree-removal service. Do not go out in active storm conditions.
4. **Stay off the road.** Even "just checking on a customer's yard" is not worth it.

## T+1 day post-clearance

1. **Drive your own service area** to assess damage. Note impassable roads, downed lines, flooding.
2. **Text customers with status update (template S8)**: *"Cleared to resume service in [area]. Checking on properties today. Will text those with damage separately."*
3. **Triage customer list** by damage level:
   - **No damage**: resume normal schedule on next visit.
   - **Minor damage** (small branches, light debris): schedule cleanup at next visit.
   - **Major damage** (large limbs, fence damage, structure impact): separate quote. These are usually insurance jobs; price accordingly.
4. **If widespread damage**: prices go up. Supply/demand. Be transparent: "Post-storm rates are 1.5× normal because the work is harder and the demand is higher. Standard rates resume after [date]."

## T+3 to T+14 days (recovery)

1. **Prioritize existing customers** over new quotes for the first 2 weeks post-storm.
2. **Bundle cleanup with regular visits.** Don't price-gouge but don't absorb 3 hours of debris removal at $75 either.
3. **Document for insurance purposes** (yours, theirs): take photos before/during/after any cleanup work.
4. **Check on elderly or single-parent customers** personally. A 5-minute "you OK?" call is worth more than a $300 cleanup job. This is how solo operators build the kind of loyalty chains that survive for decades.

## Documentation

- **Per-storm file** in `state/hurricane-history/`: name, dates, customer impact, hours worked, revenue captured.
- **Customer-by-customer log**: which prep work was done, what damage occurred, what was billed.
- **Lessons-learned** section at the bottom of each storm file. What worked, what didn't.

## What NEVER happens in hurricane mode

- **We don't price-gouge existing customers.** Post-storm rates apply to NEW quotes only.
- **We don't work in unsafe conditions.** 30 mph sustained = no outdoor work.
- **We don't abandon customers who can't pay.** A 60-day payment plan on a $500 cleanup is fine. A "we won't come out until you pay last month" is not.
- **We don't lie about damage to extract insurance money.** If a customer asks "should I file a claim for this?" — answer honestly.

## The hurricane-prep service offering

Per `content/templates/quote-template.md`, hurricane prep is a defined service:

> **HURRICANE PREP (June–November)**
> - Pre-storm yard walk + vulnerable branch removal
> - Secured items list coordinated with homeowner
> - Rate: $[XX] per activation, $[XX]/hr after first hour

Customers can buy this as a one-off or as part of a "Plus" tier. **The activation fee covers a 30-minute walk + minor branch work.** Anything major is hourly. This pricing structure makes hurricane prep sustainable for you and affordable for customers.

## Activation checklist (when you read "Tropical Storm [Name] forms")

- [ ] Update task tracker: "HURRICANE MODE: [Name]"
- [ ] Email recurring customers (template T7)
- [ ] Identify at-risk customers (3+ trees, near-coastal, etc.)
- [ ] Block the T-1 prep visits on calendar
- [ ] Pre-stage supplies (tarps, pruners, fuel)
- [ ] Charge all batteries to 100%
- [ ] Top off vehicle fuel
- [ ] Cash on hand for 5 days
- [ ] Notify family of storm plan
- [ ] Bookmark NHC: nhc.noaa.gov

## Why this runbook matters

Hurricane mode is the single biggest reputational risk a lawn-care operator has. Customers who get prepped in advance feel cared for. Customers who get ghosted post-storm switch operators. The customers you take care of in the storm are the customers who refer everyone in their neighborhood for the next 5 years.

It's also the single biggest revenue spike. A 6-week recovery period post-storm, with prices 1.5× normal, can add 20% to annual revenue. Done right, hurricane mode is the best customer-acquisition + revenue tool in the operator's kit. Done wrong, it's the end of the business.
