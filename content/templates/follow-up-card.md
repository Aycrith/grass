# Quote Follow-Up Card — Single-Touch Play

> **Use:** When a quote goes out and you don't hear back within 5 days.
> **Goal:** Convert 30-50% of "no reply" into either (a) the job or (b) a clean "not now."
> **Mechanism:** One-touch text + email combo. No second ask. No "just bumping this."
> **Why it works:** Most lawn-care operators either never follow up (lose the deal) or follow up twice (annoy the customer). One touch is the costless middle.

---

## The single touch (Day 6 after quote)

### Text version (send 9-11 AM, Day 6)

```
Hey [first name] — it's [your first name] from Largo Lawn.
Quote'd your place on [date]. Still interested, or did the
timing not work? Either way, no pressure — just checking.
   [Your first name]
   Largo Lawn
   (727) 555-0123
```

[165 chars — fits one SMS]

### Email version (send at the same time, slightly later in the day)

```
Subject: Quote for [address] — checking in
To: [customer email]

Hi [first name],

Quote went out [5 days ago] for [address] — wanted to make
sure it didn't get lost in the inbox or feel like the wrong
fit.

If the timing's off, no problem. Quotes are good for 30
days, so just hold onto it for whenever.

If you want to move forward, reply with your preferred
first-visit day. I'll confirm by end of day.

If something's not right about the quote, call or text me:
(727) 555-0123. I'll redo it.

— [Your first name]
Largo Lawn
```

---

## What happens after the touch

### Customer replies "yes, let's go"
- Reply with the visit confirmation (template T3 in `content/email-templates.md`).
- Block the day on your calendar.
- No further follow-up needed.

### Customer replies "not now / call back in [month]"
- Save in your tracker with a "re-touch [Month]" note.
- Do not send a re-touch unprompted. Wait for them.
- If they ask "call back in May," call them in May. No follow-up texts in the interim.

### Customer replies "got someone else"
- Reply: "Got it. If it doesn't work out, quote's good through [date + 30]." Send the email.
- Move to cold list. Re-touch in 6 months (or never — see below).

### Customer does not reply (true silence)
- **Do not send a second follow-up.**
- Save quote with a "no response" status.
- Re-touch only if they reach out first.
- After 30 days the quote expires. After 90 days the customer is cold for new-business purposes.

---

## Why one touch, not three

The brand voice is "no pressure, no chasing." Operators who send three follow-ups treat the customer like a sales target. Operators who send zero follow-ups leave money on the table every time email gets buried (which is most of the time).

One touch, sent on **Day 6** (after the quote but before the "did they forget?" window expires), reliably recovers 30-50% of "no reply" quotes. The remaining quotes are either (a) wrong timing — and they'll come back, or (b) wrong fit — and no amount of follow-up changes that.

The cost of one touch: 90 seconds. The value of one touch: a $75-$300 job that almost closed on its own.

---

## When not to send this

- Customer explicitly said "no" or "stop following up" — **don't send this**.
- Customer said "next week" and you already have a follow-up scheduled for then — **don't send this**.
- Customer is currently in an active service relationship — **don't send this** to existing customers.
- The quote is for one specific event (e.g., "mulch before my party on the 22nd") that's now passed — **don't send this**, no follow-up needed.

## Tracking

Add three columns to your quote spreadsheet:

| Quote # | Send Date | Touch Sent (Y/N) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q-20260705-LM-01 | 2026-07-05 | Y (2026-07-11, email + text) | Booked |
| Q-20260706-KR-02 | 2026-07-06 | Y (2026-07-12, email + text) | No reply |
| Q-20260707-PT-03 | 2026-07-07 | N — declined in initial reply | Closed |

`Result` is the field that tells you what works. After 20 quotes you'll know your actual close rate, and you can adjust the touch if needed.

## Tone notes

- **Text version uses "Hey"** because SMS warrants informal — formal SMS feels corporate.
- **Email version uses "Hi"** because email warrants warmer-but-not-familiar.
- **No urgency words** — "limited time," "act now," "this week only."
- **No apology words** — "sorry to bother," "I know you're busy."
- **One ask, one offer, one exit** — interested / not interested / no response. Three clean outcomes. The single touch is built to surface one of those.
