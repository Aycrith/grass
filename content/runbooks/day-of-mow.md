# Runbook 1 — Day-of-Mow Routine

> **Use:** Standing operating procedure for any routine mow day. Read this the night before your first 5 jobs, then again monthly.
> **Scope:** Solo-operator, recurring weekly clients (the dominant workload).
> **Goal:** Hit the property, do excellent work, leave nothing for the customer to chase, leave nothing for you to forget.

---

## T-1 (night before)

1. **Check the route.** What's the order tomorrow? Cluster ZIPs. Start east, work west, end closest to home.
2. **Check the weather.** Real rain? Wind > 20 mph sustained? If yes, text all impacted customers template S3 (cancel) or T6 (reschedule).
3. **Check the schedule.** Any new customers? Get the gate code, dog status, and side-yard access notes from the spreadsheet (one column per property).
4. **Lay out tomorrow's kit.** Mower fueled, line trimmed with fresh line, edger gassed, blower battery > 80%. One full spare trimmer line spool.

## T-0 (start of day, 7:30 AM standard)

1. **Fuel + water.** Fill mower tank. Fill water bottle. Florida in summer = 1L/hr sweat.
2. **Phone check.** Any new texts? Any "do you have a moment" messages from the night before? Reply within 15 minutes of starting the day.
3. **First-job confirmation.** Text template S4 ("On the way — ETA [X]") 30 minutes before first arrival.

## At the property

### Arrival (5 minutes)

1. **Text customer (if home)**: *"On site — starting now. Will text when done."* (template S5 variant)
2. **Visual survey from the curb.** Anything new since the last visit? Note in the property card. Examples: new garden bed, low-hanging branches, dog poop, kid's toys.
3. **Pickup before mow.** Walk the property. Trash, branches, dog waste, toys. 5 minutes here saves 30 minutes later. Note: don't enter side yards until gates are verified.

### Mow (30-60 minutes typical, varies by lot size)

1. **Mow in alternating directions week-to-week** to reduce wheel ruts.
2. **Cut height:** St. Augustine 3.5-4", Bahia 3-3.5", Bermuda 2-2.5". Don't bag unless customer explicitly asks (it's free fertilizer; bagging removes nitrogen from the property).
3. **Two passes if very tall.** First pass high (4"+), second pass at normal height. Better than one scalping.
4. **Mow edges second.** Sidewalks, curbs, drives — the edger is faster and cleaner than the mower for these.

### Edge + Trim (15-30 minutes)

1. **Mechanical edger along hard surfaces.** Curbs, sidewalks, driveways. Skip bed lines (string trimmer does those).
2. **String trim along bed lines, fence lines, tree rings, and around posts.** Trimmer line held at 90° to the surface, slightly tilted forward. Don't scalp the dirt — leave 1/4" of grass.
3. **Edge definitions where soil has crept onto turf.** Done with edger only on hard surfaces. Bed-line cleanup done with trimmer.

### Blow + Finish (10-15 minutes)

1. **Blow all hard surfaces.** Sidewalks, drives, curbs, patios, porches. Grass clippings on hardscape look unfinished.
2. **Blow clippings AWAY from beds and into the lawn** where they'll decompose as mulch.
3. **Pickup round 2.** Any debris missed earlier? Snapped branches, dog waste, large sticks. Remove from property.

### Walk-the-yard (5 minutes)

1. **Customer's-eye view.** Walk the property the way the customer walks it — from the front door, from the driveway, from the back porch. Anything look unfinished?
2. **Gate check.** All gates closed (not just latched — closed, so dogs/kids can't push through).
3. **Door / hose** left as found. Trash cans, garden hoses, anything moved — back to where it was.

### Departure (5 minutes)

1. **Text customer** (if not home): *"Done — looks great. Invoice attached / hand-delivered."* (template T3 variant)
2. **Drop the review-magnet card** in the door or hand to the customer if home.
3. **Move to next property.**

## After last job (T+8-9 hours)

1. **Equipment cleanup.** Knock dried grass off the mower deck with a stick (not a hose — water on a hot engine is bad). Empty the bag if you used one. Wipe trimmer line spool cover.
2. **Equipment check.** Anything need sharpening, refilling, replacing? Note for tomorrow.
3. **Invoice + receipt.** Invoice emailed same-day (template in `content/templates/invoice-template.md`). Receipt created if paid.
4. **Log the day.** Spreadsheet: visits, total invoiced, total collected, jobs booked, follow-ups needed.
5. **Quick cash deposit.** Venmo / Zelle / Square transfers throughout the day avoid a "trip to the bank" mental overhead. Reconcile at week's end.

## Common pitfalls (don't do these)

- **Don't blow clippings into the street.** It's litter, it looks trashy, it gets you fined in some HOAs.
- **Don't edge wet grass.** Slippery, uneven cut, line breaks faster.
- **Don't trim in the same direction every week.** Switch it up; otherwise the grass leans.
- **Don't leave the gate open.** Even if the customer said "leave it open." Dogs escape. Kids wander. Liability is yours.
- **Don't answer the phone mid-cut.** Pull the trimmer line out of the spool first, set it down, then answer. The 30 seconds saved aren't worth a dropped call becoming a voicemail from a customer who heard a chainsaw.

## What "excellent work" looks like

1. **The grass looks like a carpet.** No wheel ruts, no scalping, no clumps of clippings.
2. **The edges look like a razor drew them.** Straight lines along curbs and walks, soft curves along bed lines.
3. **The hardscape is clean.** No clippings, no tire marks, no trimmer line debris.
4. **The customer walks out and says "wow."** Not "looks good" — **"wow."**

That's the bar. Every mow, every time.
